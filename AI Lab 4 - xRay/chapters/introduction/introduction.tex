\section{Introduction}

Galaxy clusters are among the most massive structures in the universe, 
providing valuable insights into both astrophysics and cosmology. 
Accurately measuring their masses is essential for studies of large-scale 
structure, galaxy evolution, and the nature of dark matter and dark energy. 
X-ray observations offer a powerful way to probe the hot intracluster medium 
and infer cluster masses.

In this work, we investigate the use of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) 
for the reconstruction of galaxy cluster masses from X-ray images, with a
particular focus on evaluating the effectiveness and uncertainty calibration
of different neural network architectures. We present the methodology adopted, 
discussing the rationale behind our model choices and training strategies. 
Finally, we compare the results of two high-performing models and critically 
assess their performance, identifying the most reliable approach for this task.
